## Final report — three approaches to review classification

We compared zero-shot and three-example few-shot prompting of the same hosted Qwen3.6-35B-A3B AWQ model with Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct fine-tuned on the prescribed 900 reviews. The hosted model has about 35B total parameters and about 3B active per token; the smaller model has about 3.09B base parameters. We chose the 3B model to meet the 2–5B guidance and leave room for training on a 16 GB T4. Four-bit QLoRA trains small adapter matrices rather than all base weights.

All approaches used the same task instruction, verbatim title/text formatting, and the same 300 test IDs. The approved demonstrations (#8849, #2359, #4447) illustrate satisfaction, a non-evaluative question, and dissatisfaction; all belong to training. The validation set was reserved and unused. No test result changed the prompts, examples, model, or learning settings. Greedy/temperature-zero decoding used an eight-token cap; thinking was disabled.

**Fine-tuned had the highest macro-F1 on this test set.** Balanced accuracy is also ordinary accuracy here because there are exactly 100 references per class. Neutral-class recall was Zero-shot: 38%; Few-shot: 44%; Fine-tuned: 70%. Compared with few-shot prompting, fine-tuning raised neutral recall from 44% to 70%, while negative recall changed from 95% to 77%. For example, review #30017 (title: 'Good') was predicted NEUTRAL rather than its POSITIVE reference. The confusion matrices and concrete errors above show the remaining mistakes. Star-derived reference labels are imperfect measures of textual sentiment, particularly for mixed reviews. Titles such as Five Stars and HTML entities were intentionally retained.

**Paired statistical comparisons.** Zero-shot vs Few-shot: exact McNemar p=0.548828 (no established difference in correctness at the 0.05 level); only the first was correct on 4 reviews and only the second on 7. Zero-shot vs Fine-tuned: exact McNemar p=0.123731 (no established difference in correctness at the 0.05 level); only the first was correct on 24 reviews and only the second on 37. Few-shot vs Fine-tuned: exact McNemar p=0.245061 (no established difference in correctness at the 0.05 level); only the first was correct on 25 reviews and only the second on 35. These three unadjusted tests are exploratory, test correctness rather than macro-F1, and do not establish general superiority or equivalence. Different base models and sizes also prevent attributing the whole difference solely to fine-tuning.

**Compute and reproducibility.** Training completed two epochs / 114 optimizer steps directly on Tesla T4, taking 8.68 minutes, with peak allocated memory 2.39 GiB and peak reserved memory 2.47 GiB. The fresh saved-adapter model generated all 300 predictions on Tesla T4 in 1.57 minutes; peak allocated memory during that run was 2.82 GiB. Thus full training and submitted-adapter inference were directly tested on a T4. Training used learning rate 0.0002, rank 16/alpha 32, fp16, batch 2 with eight-step accumulation, six warm-up steps, seed 43, response-only loss, and no truncation. The hosted requests were completed from a local Windows client during the Colab outage, with two requests in flight. They were imported here only after verifying immutable file hashes, every request, every raw response, and the prescribed IDs. Hosted and local timings include different hardware, concurrency, networking, and compilation costs, so they are not a controlled speed benchmark. Prompting avoids training setup; the adapter requires upfront training but permits inference on the smaller local model.

**An agent decision investigated.** Unsloth initially substituted a prequantized repository and ignored the requested original-base revision. We caught this before training, required the exact original model name, and pinned its revision. The earlier interrupted runtime's adapter was not assumed valid or complete; this submission uses a fresh, fully audited training run. The final ZIP was extracted separately, its original base and matching tokenizer were loaded afresh, and its adapter was attached before generating the submitted predictions and three actual prompt examples. The final checks match the ZIP's adapter hashes to inference and match the prediction-file hash to these reported metrics.
